## Before the interview

- [ ] Re-read the research goal and pick 8–12 questions from the bank below
- [ ] Test your recording and transcription (e.g. Otter.ai or Zoom)
- [ ] Ask a colleague to take notes so you can focus on listening
- [ ] Prepare the consent form and incentive

## Script

### 1. Intro (5 min)

> Thanks so much for making time today. I'm [name], and I work on [product]. We're trying to understand how people [topic], so there are no right or wrong answers. We're not testing you.
>
> I'd like to record this so I don't have to scribble notes. The recording stays inside our team. Is that OK?
>
> Please be completely honest. You won't hurt my feelings, and critical feedback is the most useful kind.

### 2. Warm-up (5 min)

- Tell me a bit about yourself and what you do.
- What does a typical day or week look like for you?

### 3. Main questions (30–40 min)

Pick from the question bank. Go from broad to specific.

### 4. Wrap-up (5 min)

- If you had a magic wand, what's one thing you'd change about [topic]?
- Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
- Can we contact you again if we have follow-up questions?

> Thank you, this was really helpful. [Explain how and when they get the incentive.]

## Question bank

### Context and background
- Walk me through the last time you [did the task].
- How did you get started with [topic]?
- Who else is involved when you [do the task]?
- How often do you [do the task]?

### Current behaviour and tools
- What tools or apps do you use for this today?
- Can you show me how you do it? (screen share)
- Why did you choose that tool?
- What did you use before? Why did you switch?
- Are there any workarounds or hacks you've come up with?

### Pain points
- What's the most frustrating part of [task]?
- Tell me about a time when [task] went wrong.
- What takes longer than it should?
- What do you wish you didn't have to do?
- What happens if this doesn't get done?

### Goals and motivation
- What does success look like for you here?
- Why is [task] important to you?
- How do you know you've done a good job?
- What would make your life easier?

### Decision making and buying
- How did you find out about [current tool]?
- Who decides which tools you use?
- What would make you switch to something new?
- What's held you back from changing in the past?
- How much do you spend on this today, in time or money?

### Emotions
- How do you feel when you [do the task]?
- What's the best part of it?
- What worries you about it?

### Follow-up probes (use any time)
- Can you tell me more about that?
- Why is that?
- What do you mean by "[their word]"?
- Can you give me an example?
- How did that make you feel?
- What happened next?

## Tips

- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time…" gives real answers; "Would you use…" gives polite guesses.
- **Avoid leading questions.** Not "Don't you find X annoying?" but "How do you feel about X?"
- **One question at a time.** Don't stack two questions in one sentence.
- **Get comfortable with silence.** Count to five before you speak again. People often fill it with their best insight.
- **Use their words.** If they say "messy", ask "What makes it messy?"
- **Don't pitch or defend your product.** You're there to learn, not to sell.
- **5–8 interviews per user group** usually reveal the main patterns.
