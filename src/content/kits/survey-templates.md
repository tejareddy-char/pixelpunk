## When to use a survey

Surveys tell you **how many** people think or do something. Interviews tell you **why**. Use surveys to measure, compare or check whether a problem you heard in interviews is common.

## Template 1: System Usability Scale (SUS)

The standard 10-question usability score. Ask after someone has used your product. Each answer is 1 (Strongly disagree) to 5 (Strongly agree).

1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the system was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the system.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

**Scoring:** For odd questions, subtract 1 from the answer. For even questions, subtract the answer from 5. Add them up and multiply by 2.5 to get a score out of 100. **68 is about average; 80+ is excellent.**

## Template 2: Net Promoter Score (NPS)

1. How likely are you to recommend [product] to a friend or colleague? (0–10)
2. What's the main reason for your score? (open text)
3. What's one thing we could do to improve? (open text)

**Scoring:** % of 9–10 answers (promoters) minus % of 0–6 answers (detractors).

## Template 3: Feature satisfaction

1. How often do you use [feature]? (Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Rarely / Never)
2. How satisfied are you with [feature]? (1–5)
3. How important is [feature] to your work? (1–5)
4. What, if anything, is frustrating about [feature]? (open text)

**Tip:** Plot importance against satisfaction. High importance + low satisfaction = fix first.

## Template 4: Validate a new idea

1. How do you currently [do the task]? (multiple choice + Other)
2. How often do you [do the task]? (frequency scale)
3. How satisfied are you with how you do it today? (1–5)
4. What's the hardest part of [the task]? (open text)
5. If a tool could [benefit], how useful would that be? (Not at all … Extremely)
6. Would you like to try it when it's ready? (Yes, with email / No)

## Template 5: Onboarding check

1. How easy was it to get started with [product]? (1–7)
2. Was anything confusing during setup? (open text)
3. What did you expect [product] to help you with? (open text)
4. Have you been able to [key action] yet? (Yes / Not yet / Not sure how)

## Rules for good questions

- **One idea per question.** Not "How fast and reliable is it?"
- **Neutral wording.** Not "How much do you love our new design?"
- **Balanced scales.** Same number of positive and negative options.
- **Give an exit.** Add "Not applicable" or "Don't know" where it fits.
- **Keep it short.** Under 10 questions and under 5 minutes gets far more completions.
- **Put demographics last**, and only ask what you'll actually use.
- **Pilot it** with 2–3 people before sending it to everyone.
